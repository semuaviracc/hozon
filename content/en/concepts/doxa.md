> *"And the Word became flesh... and we have seen his glory (δόξα)..." — John 1:14*

**Doxa** (δόξα) is not praise or applause offered by creation to its Creator, like an audience clapping for a performer on a stage. It is something far more fundamental: God unveiled and made visible. When Scripture speaks of "the glory of God," it means not the human reaction to Him, but His very self becoming visible.

When John wrote, *"We have seen his glory,"* he was not describing a dazzling physical light, but the Living One fully revealed in Christ. To see the glory is to see God — not to see an effect of His presence.

Doxa is the "radiance" side of unveiling, paired with truth (*Aletheia*): the one guarantees that what is unveiled is true, the other ensures that what is true is truly seen. Humanity is called to reflect this glory as an image — a witness that radiates, not a source that kindles its own light.
