> *"Again Jesus spoke to them, saying, 'I am the light (φῶς) of the world...'" — John 8:12*

**Phōs** (φῶς) is the light that unveils — the Living One making Himself visible. Plato once pictured truth as a sun the soul must laboriously ascend toward, climbing out of a cave of shadows. The light here moves in exactly the opposite direction: it descends to meet us, rather than waiting to be climbed.

When God said *"let there be light"* on the first day — long before the sun existed — this was not the kindling of some object, but the self-unveiling of the Living One Himself. That is why darkness is not a rival power equal to light; it is merely space not yet touched by unveiling. *"The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it."*

Yet to shine is not automatically to be seen, for the organ that receives this light is the eye of the heart, not the physical eye. When the Light Himself finally walked among people in Christ, some still did not recognize Him — not because His light was dim, but because their hearts had not yet been enlightened.
